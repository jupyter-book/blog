In the first three months of my work as a JupyterHub and Jupyter Book community manager, I mainly focused on getting to know the people and the projects. 
Starting this month, I am taking action to support the work of the two communities. Here is what I am working on.

## JupyterHub: Organizing the HubDash and the User's survey

On 30 November and 1 December 2026 we will host the [2{sup}`nd` HubDash](https://github.com/jupyterhub/team-compass/issues/926), after a successful [first edition](https://github.com/jupyterhub/team-compass/issues/811). 
The HubDash is a two-day online event where we will work on various tasks to enhance the support for JupyterHub and its community.

One of the main activities during the HubDash will be the analysis of the outcomes of a **JupyterHub User's survey** we are creating. 
The aims of the survey are to identify new potential area for development and to collect use cases, as requested by users, so that they can learn from each other — and we can learn from them! 
We are working on the survey during our [Collaboration Cafes](https://compass.hub.jupyter.org/meetings/collab-cafe/2026/).

To get the initial conversation started, we **began with a draft** that I had prepared.
I intentionally added more questions than what we would ultimately need because the idea was to create a large pool of possible questions that we could choose from and refine together.
I also included a relatively large proportion of multiple-choice questions to make the survey quick and easy to complete while still collecting the information we need.

During our **first Collaboration Cafe** of the month (1 September 2026), we spent about five minutes at the beginning reading through the draft. 
Some people added new questions, while others suggested changes to the existing ones. 
We then went through the survey together, **discussing the initial sections and questions**.

After this first round of discussion, we sent the survey out for some **initial feedback**. 
One of the main pieces of feedback was that the survey was too long - as expected! 
In addition, another useful piece of feedback was that the open questions allowed for answers that were too broad. 
So, instead of asking open questions like: _What lessons did you learn?_ we changed to: _What were the top three lessons you learned?_.
This change should make the question not only faster to answer, but should also make the answers easier to analyze.

During the **second Collaboration Cafe** on 15 September 2026, we took another big step forward with the survey: we **reduced the number of sections** from eight to four!
The survey now includes a section about the deployments (covering both hardware and configuration), as well as more social and experience-focused questions.
We are continuing to work on the details of the questions. 

In the next month, we will continue to work through the **details of the individual questions**, making them as clear and focused as possible and removing anything that does not fit within the scope of the survey.
Then, we will send it to a few people for review. 
We will incorporate their feedback and make the final adjustments before **sending the survey out** widely.

This is our **timeline** for the coming months:
- Oct 6: Finalize questionnaire draft + send to 2-3 responders/reviewers
- Oct 20: Adjust questionnaire based on feedback + send out to contacts and post on social media
- Nov 3: HubDash Planning
- Nov 17: HubDash Planning
- Nov 30 - Dec 1: HubDash!


This year, the HubDash will be **in collaboration with** [The Turing Way Book Dash](https://book.the-turing-way.org/community-handbook/bookdash/), the group who created the concept.
The two communities will have joint sections where we will introduce ourselves and discuss the achievements we have made.
We will then have separate breakout rooms where we will work on the analysis of the survey mentioned above (and other tasks!) while they update and enhance [The Turing Way](https://book.the-turing-way.org/), a handbook for "reproducible, ethical and collaborative data science".

The **registration** form for the HubDash will be available in **October**. Stay tuned!



## Jupyter Book & MyST: Changing gear!

In the Jupyter Book & MyST community we are experiencing an exciting shift in gears! 
Since the beginning of September, we have started to organize our work differently. 
We now work in **two-week iterations**.
That is, we organize our work into tasks that can be accomplished within a two-week time frame. 

New iterations start every second Tuesday, when we have a **30-minute meeting** to share our experiences from the previous two weeks and review the results.
During the same meeting, we also plan the tasks for the next two weeks based on what remains to be done and what we want to achieve next. 

In addition, we continue to meet every Monday for the [working sessions](https://jupyterbook.org/events/#working-sessions), where we **share technical expertise and suggestions, and provide input** to one another.
These meetings have a very nice energy and the developers are genuinely excited about improving Jupyter Book & MyST! 

To keep track of our progress we created a [GitHub Project](https://github.com/orgs/jupyter-book/projects/5/views/1).
For every task, we create an issue, which will be closed when the corresponding pull request is merged. 
The challenge in this process is to properly implement the principle of **"divide and conquer"**, that is, to divide larger tasks into smaller tasks that are achievable within two weeks! 

On my side, **I always learn a lot** and I feel like with every meeting I take a small step forward in understanding the technical aspects of these projects — which is super interesting!

But beyond learning, I am also finding my way to **contribute** to the projects.
I am looking for ways to [improve the relatioships](https://github.com/jupyter-book/jupyter-book/issues/2671)  between the two websites 
[jupyterbook.org](https://jupyterbook.org/) and 
[mystmd.org](https://mystmd.org/) (I talked about the two websites in my [previous post](./03-cm-jb-myst-docs)). 
I would also love to highlight of **MyST's special features**, such as cross-referencing and embedding solved computations. 
And I would like to work with the developers **to agree on and document** the terminology used across the two projects, explain how the two tools relate to each other, and provide more context around the key concepts.

I hope that this will be **useful for new contributors**, who might benefit from clearer documentation and additional context.
And I hope that this will be useful for **users** as well, especially for those who aspire to transition from users to contributors.
